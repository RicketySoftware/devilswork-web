How to Play

1. The Premise of the Game

At the start of the story your character awakes with no memories in Hell, held captive by a grotesque and indignant devil.

As you slowly remember who you are, you will be asked to randomly draw three cards (Heart, Kin and Class), which determine your character's personality, physical traits and skills; you obtain two different Powers, a weapon attack, your roll incompetencies, and a variety of potential calamities.

You will be asked to choose a name, a backstory and your sin against the devil Larry. You will then be "inducted" into the company, and sent on your first job to undertake the devil's work of granting a wish for a client in exchange for their soul. Succeed and you are 'let go' from Larry's employment; fail the job and he takes your souls for his collection as compensation.

2. Gameplay Modes and Turn Order

There are three gameplay modes:

  • Exploration: the Story-Master describes the scene and players take turns describing their characters' responses, in a civilised first-come, first-served order.
  • Diplomacy: conversations are conducted like radio-plays where you speak as your character; at the end of the conversation the Story-Master determines the difficulty of the roll based on the quality of the conversation.
  • Combat: opposing sides take turns as a group, enemies always go first, unless they are ambushed. When your team attacks, the order is determined by the player with the Turn-Manager card, who can command when a player takes their turn. This card rotates around the players after the combat ends.

When it is your turn in combat, you can do three different things: move once, take one action and speak, in any order. When speaking, you can communicate information to other characters, however you cannot engage in "diplomacy" such as flirting or intimidating without using your action. You can move anywhere within one room on your turn, but if there is an obstacle or hostile in your way you will need to roll to traverse it (Sloth). If you fail this roll you are halted by the obstacle.

3. Actions

You have actions such as attacks and powers that are detailed on your character cards, unique to your Kin and Class. Power charges can be spent to use Powers; these are earned, through good roleplay or clever thinking at the Story-Master's discretion.

Card-based actions are not affected by your incompetencies so you do not add anything to your roll when you roll to hit the target number on your card.

However, common actions are affected by your incompetencies, which are detailed on your Kin card. See the chart to find the relevant type of incompetency for a common action:

Scrawniness: The weakness of your muscles - Throwing, Lifting, Tackling, Grappling...

Sloth: The Sluggishness of your Body - Climbing, Swimming, Sprinting, Acrobatics, Dodging...

Infirmity: The Frailty of your Spirit - Breath Holding, Withstanding Poison, Staying conscious...

Repulsivity: The Vulgarity of your Words - Intimidating, Seducing, Distracting, Insulting...

Obliviosity: The Dullness of your Senses - Spotting details, Reading people, Reacting on the fly...

Stupidity: The Deficiency of your Intellect - Lock Picking, Investigating, Memory, Research...

Additionally, you can spend a Power Charge to perform a Stunt, where you may take as many 'Scrawniness' and 'Sloth' based common actions as you like on your turn, however you are stopped if you fail an action.

You will meet other weirdos during the story, helping them may result in earning a cursed item card. Cursed Items are similar to powers, but cost nothing to use, however you have to roll and beat the target number detailed on the card to avoid activating the curse. These can be used only once.

4. Die Rolls

Your action success or failure depends on whether you hit or beat the action's Target Score with your Roll Total.

To get your Roll Total, roll the die and combine the result with your relevant incompetency.

E.g. [Rolled a 7] + [-3 Sloth] = Roll Total of 4

The Target Score is a number that can either be predetermined by the job-module, or determined by a contest roll against the Story-Master, who adds an appropriate bonus or penalty to their roll depending on the action difficulty.

Rolling a natural 12 is always a success.[cite: 1] Rolling a natural 1 is a "Calamity" which is an automatic failure. When this happens to you, consult your Heart card to see what horribleness occurs to your character.

5. Health

You start with twelve Health Points, if they fall to zero you die.[cite: 1] Your character can have a condition inflicted upon them, such as deafened, confused, intoxicated, charmed etc. When this happens, you have to roleplay the condition on your turn and take the appropriate actions. Good roleplay, while "kneecapping" your own character, earns a Power Charge. If you describe your character's death well, you get a chance to earn a Power Charge for your new character, who will be introduced at the next sensible point in the story. If all of the players have died at least once, you have failed the job and Larry keeps your souls.
