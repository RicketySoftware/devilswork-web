How to Play

1. The Premise of the Game

At the start of the story your character awakes with no memories in Hell, held captive by a grotesque and indignant devil.[cite: 1]

As you slowly remember who you are, you will be asked to randomly draw three cards (Heart, Kin and Class), which determine your character's personality, physical traits and skills; you obtain two different Powers, a weapon attack, your roll incompetencies, and a variety of potential calamities.[cite: 1]

You will be asked to choose a name, a backstory and your sin against the devil Larry.[cite: 1] You will then be "inducted" into the company, and sent on your first job to undertake the devil's work of granting a wish for a client in exchange for their soul.[cite: 1] Succeed and you are 'let go' from Larry's employment; fail the job and he takes your souls for his collection as compensation.[cite: 1]

2. Gameplay Modes and Turn Order

There are three gameplay modes:[cite: 1]

  • Exploration: the Story-Master describes the scene and players take turns describing their characters' responses, in a civilised first-come, first-served order.[cite: 1]
  • Diplomacy: conversations are conducted like radio-plays where you speak as your character; at the end of the conversation the Story-Master determines the difficulty of the roll based on the quality of the conversation.[cite: 1]
  • Combat: opposing sides take turns as a group, enemies always go first, unless they are ambushed.[cite: 1] When your team attacks, the order is determined by the player with the Turn-Manager card, who can command when a player takes their turn.[cite: 1] This card rotates around the players after the combat ends.[cite: 1]

When it is your turn in combat, you can do three different things: move once, take one action and speak, in any order.[cite: 1] When speaking, you can communicate information to other characters, however you cannot engage in "diplomacy" such as flirting or intimidating without using your action.[cite: 1] You can move anywhere within one room on your turn, but if there is an obstacle or hostile in your way you will need to roll to traverse it (Sloth).[cite: 1] If you fail this roll you are halted by the obstacle.[cite: 1]

3. Actions

You have actions such as attacks and powers that are detailed on your character cards, unique to your Kin and Class.[cite: 1] Power charges can be spent to use Powers; these are earned, through good roleplay or clever thinking at the Story-Master's discretion.[cite: 1]

Card-based actions are not affected by your incompetencies so you do not add anything to your roll when you roll to hit the target number on your card.[cite: 1]

However, common actions are affected by your incompetencies, which are detailed on your Kin card.[cite: 1] See the chart to find the relevant type of incompetency for a common action:[cite: 1]

Scrawniness: The weakness of your muscles - Throwing, Lifting, Tackling, Grappling...[cite: 1]

Sloth: The Sluggishness of your Body - Climbing, Swimming, Sprinting, Acrobatics, Dodging...[cite: 1]

Infirmity: The Frailty of your Spirit - Breath Holding, Withstanding Poison, Staying conscious...[cite: 1]

Repulsivity: The Vulgarity of your Words - Intimidating, Seducing, Distracting, Insulting...[cite: 1]

Obliviosity: The Dullness of your Senses - Spotting details, Reading people, Reacting on the fly...[cite: 1]

Stupidity: The Deficiency of your Intellect - Lock Picking, Investigating, Memory, Research...[cite: 1]

Additionally, you can spend a Power Charge to perform a Stunt, where you may take as many 'Scrawniness' and 'Sloth' based common actions as you like on your turn, however you are stopped if you fail an action.[cite: 1]

You will meet other weirdos during the story, helping them may result in earning a cursed item card.[cite: 1] Cursed Items are similar to powers, but cost nothing to use, however you have to roll and beat the target number detailed on the card to avoid activating the curse.[cite: 1] These can be used only once.[cite: 1]

4. Die Rolls

Your action success or failure depends on whether you hit or beat the action's Target Score with your Roll Total.[cite: 1]

To get your Roll Total, roll the die and combine the result with your relevant incompetency.[cite: 1]

E.g. [Rolled a 7] + [-3 Sloth] = Roll Total of 4[cite: 1]

The Target Score is a number that can either be predetermined by the job-module, or determined by a contest roll against the Story-Master, who adds an appropriate bonus or penalty to their roll depending on the action difficulty.[cite: 1]

Rolling a natural 12 is always a success.[cite: 1] Rolling a natural 1 is a "Calamity" which is an automatic failure.[cite: 1] When this happens to you, consult your Heart card to see what horribleness occurs to your character.[cite: 1]

5. Health

You start with twelve Health Points, if they fall to zero you die.[cite: 1] Your character can have a condition inflicted upon them, such as deafened, confused, intoxicated, charmed etc.[cite: 1] When this happens, you have to roleplay the condition on your turn and take the appropriate actions.[cite: 1] Good roleplay, while "kneecapping" your own character, earns a Power Charge.[cite: 1] If you describe your character's death well, you get a chance to earn a Power Charge for your new character, who will be introduced at the next sensible point in the story.[cite: 1] If all of the players have died at least once, you have failed the job and Larry keeps your souls.[cite: 1]
